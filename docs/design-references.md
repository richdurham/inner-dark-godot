# Design references and synthesis

These games are reference points for particular design problems, not feature checklists. Their player roles, scopes, and interfaces differ from The Inner Dark.

## Useful precedents

| Game | Relevant pattern | What it suggests for The Inner Dark | Important difference / risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Fallen London / StoryNexus](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/storynexus-developer-diary-2-fewer-spreadsheets-less-swearing) | Storylets are small narrative units whose availability depends on changeable qualities; choices return to the shared world by explicitly changing those qualities. Failbetter frames this as a compromise between fixed branching trees and a full world model. Their engineering notes also warn that too many interacting qualities make sequences fragile, and that each quality needs consistent meaning. | Let simulation-derived conditions, earned capabilities, relationships, and inventory qualities open compact opportunities. Have each choice state its important effects, then let later storylets respond to the changed state. Prefer coherent, reusable storylets over enormous branching trees. | Fallen London is a text-first, long-running browser game with its own pacing and action economy. Do not copy its grind or monetization. Keep this project's tag/quality set small, consistently defined, and connected to authoritative state. |
| [Six Ages: Ride Like the Wind](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/making-six-ages-for-ios-a-retrospective) | Narrative choices are supported by a politico-economic model; changing qualities affect later storylets. Advisors have personalities and can pursue agendas. The designer also simplified exact labor/land accounting into broader Ventures. | Let authored situations read simulation state and tags, then alter resources, relationships, capabilities, and later access. Keep background production abstract where exact allocation does not create a player choice. | The player leads a clan. The Inner Dark player is an individual and should influence community responses through personal actions, not govern the settlement. |
| [Suzerain](https://www.articy.com/en/showcase/suzerain/) | A long choice-driven narrative tracks variables such as economy, budget, public opinion, unrest, relationships, and policy. Consequences accumulate across chains of decisions and are reflected in dialogue and reports. | Make choices affect several understandable dimensions and let later scenes respond to those accumulated conditions. Use state summaries to communicate consequences. | The player is a head of state. The Inner Dark needs to preserve personal scale and environmental interaction rather than expose a national dashboard. |
| [Shadows of Doubt](https://colepowered.com/shadows-of-doubt-devblog-2-finding-the-game/) | A procedural city and citizen simulation support first-person investigation. The developer describes recording citizen activity and memories so clues can be discovered later, while also noting the scale and data burden of simulating it. | Put simulation consequences into places, people, schedules, and discoverable evidence. Make investigations emerge from a world state the player can inspect and act upon. | Fully recording every citizen action is expensive and can overwhelm the project. Use persistent roster identities plus aggregate background capability; retain fine-grained traces only when they support a relevant investigation or story. |
| [S.T.A.L.K.E.R.: Shadow of Chernobyl](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/interview-inside-the-ai-of-i-s-t-a-l-k-e-r-i-) | Its A-Life system used detailed online NPC behavior near the player and simpler offline behavior elsewhere; offline characters still pursued goals and could generate reportable events. | Keep NPCs persistent and goal-oriented while changing simulation detail by relevance. Summarize consequential offscreen events for the player. | Its described online/offline boundary is geographic and local; The Inner Dark also needs broader settlement, planetary, regional, and Solar System cadences. |
| [Victoria 3](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-76-performance) | Simulation tasks run at different frequencies (including daily, weekly, monthly, and yearly) and have explicit dependencies. The developers discuss the performance cost of less frequent tasks that also include frequent work. | Use one world clock with scheduled tasks by scale and explicit update dependencies. Profile expensive catch-up tasks and avoid doing unnecessary daily work for inactive locations. | Its player is managing a country, and its detailed economic simulation is much broader than this project's intended player-facing view. |
| [Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord](https://www.taleworlds.com/en/Games/Bannerlord/Blog/14) | Map parties transport goods between producing villages, towns, and markets; disruptions and aid have knock-on effects on settlement prosperity. | Connect places through flows and travel so a personal delivery, trade, or disruption can affect broader conditions without direct colony management. | Its world map foregrounds parties and resource information; The Inner Dark should show only the detail useful to the player's current scale and knowledge. |

## Synthesis for The Inner Dark

The intended combination is:

1. **World simulation creates pressures.** Production, demand, movement, and community capability determine conditions such as a strained service or a market shortage.
2. **NPCs pursue their own concerns.** A persistent roster and aggregate capability model let background problems progress without simulating every individual continuously. A relevant existing NPC becomes visible when the player can encounter or affect the situation.
3. **The player explores and investigates at personal scale.** Locations, interactions, physical clues, schedules, and conversations reveal only part of the simulation. The player may help, trade for profit, build goodwill, gather evidence, exploit an opportunity, or walk away.
4. **Choices update authoritative state.** Actions validate skill, access, items, money, timing, and NPC acceptance. They can alter a work order, flow rate, relationship, or evidence record.
5. **Tags connect simulation and story.** Condition tags derive from world state; capability and relationship tags come from authored setup or validated actions. Storylets and mission access can check these tags alongside location, time, knowledge, and resources. Keep each tag's meaning consistent, show the practical effects of important choices, and avoid large interdependent tag chains.
6. **Resolution follows the player's scale.** Local detail advances when relevant; larger scales update more coarsely. Important offscreen outcomes persist, while routine activity is aggregated.

This combines authored narrative structure with systemic consequences and 3D investigation. It does not require a fully simulated biography for every citizen or a full-colony management interface. Start with one NPC-owned situation, one player-scale encounter, a few state-changing choices, and clear consequences at both local and settlement level.

## Failbetter design notes

- [StoryNexus developer diary: “fewer spreadsheets, less swearing”](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/storynexus-developer-diary-2-fewer-spreadsheets-less-swearing) explains quality-based narrative as storylets whose appearance is controlled by changeable state, with each choice returning the player to a shared stateful world.
- [“Don’t Poke That: Narrative Engineering Principles”](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/dont-poke-that-narrative-engineering-principles-part-1) warns that too many qualities controlling ordered sequences make content difficult to reason about, and that reusing a quality inconsistently causes authoring and exploit problems.
- [“Redesigning the Story Page”](https://www.failbettergames.com/news/redesigning-the-story-page) describes the loop in which gaining qualities opens stories and stories provide more qualities. Its practical implication is to make opportunity requirements and choice effects legible to the player.

For the proposed local semantic layer, see [semantic world narrative design](semantic-world-narrative.md). It uses embedding retrieval to suggest connections among existing world records, then validates relationships and effects against structured game state. A research precedent for retrieving natural-language memories by relevance, recency, and importance is the [Generative Agents paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442); its agent-memory architecture is inspiration for context retrieval, not authority for game-state changes.
